### ADR-001 — Theme: evaluate stocks against Graham and Buffett criteria
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Context:** Need a domain with clear, explainable criteria so the agent design (not model capability) is the demo.
**Decision:** Evaluate companies against Benjamin Graham's defensive-investor criteria and Warren Buffett's published principles.
**Trade-off:** Classic criteria are dated (Graham's are from the 1970s), so few modern stocks pass all of them. Treated as a talking point, not a flaw.

### ADR-002 — Universe: S&P 500 only
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Context:** Free structured financial data (SEC EDGAR) covers US filers only.
**Decision:** v1 covers S&P 500 constituents. Other tickers return "outside v1 scope".
**Trade-off:** No ASX or international coverage; would need paid or different data sources.

### ADR-003 — Output is an explained scorecard, never a verdict
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Context:** Must stay a research tool, not financial advice.
**Decision:** Output is "Graham X/7 · Buffett Y/8", with each criterion's figure, threshold, source, date and plain-English explanation. No buy/sell language, no "undervalued".
**Trade-off:** Less punchy than a rating, but defensible and transparent.

### ADR-004 — The platform does not adjudicate between criteria and analysts
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Context:** The two rankings will often disagree.
**Decision:** Show the criteria ranking and the analyst ranking side by side with no linking commentary. Users draw their own conclusions. Any agreement/disagreement statistics stay in the README/evaluation notes, not the UI.
**Trade-off:** Gives up a headline claim in exchange for neutrality and credibility.

### ADR-005 — Deterministic scoring in Python; LLM for explanation and qualitative judgement only
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Context:** Graham criteria and Buffett's quantitative tests are arithmetic. LLMs are unreliable at arithmetic and cost money.
**Decision:** All numeric criteria are computed by Python scripts. LLMs (a) explain results and (b) assess the four qualitative Buffett criteria from 10-K text, with mandatory citations.
**Trade-off:** Less "AI" on the surface; stronger story about choosing the right tool for each step. The agentic behaviour lives in planning, filing retrieval, qualitative judgement and the critic.

### ADR-006 — Two modes: discovery and ticker entry
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Decision:** Discovery mode shows both rankings for the S&P 500; ticker mode analyses any S&P 500 ticker.
**Trade-off:** The full S&P 500 scorecard is cheap (no LLM); the LLM analysis will be pre-computed for a featured set to stay at $0 (see open questions).

### ADR-007 — Data sources: EDGAR for fundamentals, Finnhub for price and analyst consensus
**Status:** Pending (Finnhub permission) · 2026-10-07
**Context:** Finnhub's free tier is personal-use only; redistribution needs written approval. EDGAR is public domain.
**Decision:** All criteria use EDGAR. Finnhub supplies current price and analyst recommendation counts. Finnhub is used freely for development and testing (personal use, within free-tier terms). **Publish gate:** no Finnhub data goes on a public URL until Finnhub has replied in writing. **Trade-off:** Licence-clean design costs two criteria in the worst case. Shows governance over convenience.

### ADR-010 — Critic step as a verification gate
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Decision:** A Critic checks that (1) every figure in the explanation matches the script output, (2) every qualitative judgement cites a filing passage, (3) no recommendation-style language appears. One retry, then show with a visible warning.

### ADR-011 — Criteria thresholds
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Context:** Graham published exact rules; Buffett published principles only.
**Decision:** Graham criteria G1–G7 kept as written in *The Intelligent Investor* (1973), except G1 size updated to revenue ≥ $1B (inflation-adjusted $100M). Buffett quantitative criteria set as documented interpretations: B1 ROE ≥ 15% in ≥ 7 of 10 yrs; B2 long-term debt ≤ 4× 3-yr avg net income; B3 operating margin positive every year and last-3-yr avg ≥ 90% of 10-yr avg; B4 owner earnings (NI + D&A − capex) positive in ≥ 8 of 10 yrs and growing. Full table in `PROCESS.md` §5.
**Trade-off:** Buffett thresholds are judgement calls. Mitigated by stating them openly in the README and by a ±20% threshold sensitivity test in the evaluation harness.

### ADR-012 — G4 dividend record: 15-year window, always "partial"
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Context:** Graham requires 20 years of uninterrupted dividends; EDGAR structured data covers ~15–17 years.
**Decision:** Test a fixed 15-year window. A pass is always labelled "partial (15 of 20 yrs)". Companies with a shorter record (recent listings, spin-offs) pass if they paid a dividend every year on record, labelled "N yrs on record". No dividend in any year = fail.
**Trade-off:** Lenient for short-record companies; transparency comes from the label rather than a stricter rule.
**Amended 2026-10-08 (correction):** "years on record" means years for which the company has annual financial data in EDGAR (its net income series), **not** years with dividend data. As first written, a company that is long listed but only recently started paying (Alphabet: financials from 2013, dividends from 2024) would have passed as "2 yrs on record". A year counts as paid only if dividends per share is above zero; if per-share data is missing for a year, dividends paid above zero is used instead, and the source is shown. Zero-valued dividend facts (Alphabet reports $0 in several years) count as not paid. A one-off non-dividend payment can still show up in the cash-paid tag (Alphabet, $47M in 2015), which is why per-share data takes priority.
**Amended 2026-10-11 (decided by Daniel):** a year with no dividend data that lies **between the company's first and last paid years** is "unknown", not unpaid. G4 then reads: any confirmed unpaid year in the window (a reported zero, or no data before the first or after the last paid year) → **fail**; otherwise any unknown year → **data_gap**, with a reason naming the years ("dividend data missing for 2011"); otherwise partial pass as before. The first and last paid years come from the company's whole history, from per-share data when the company has any positive per-share dividend and from cash paid only when it has none (Alphabet's one-off $47M cash-paid tag in 2015 must not turn 2016 to 2023 into years "between payments"). Years before the first paid year stay unpaid, so Alphabet, Amazon and Tesla are unchanged. Effect over the index: one label moved (Dollar General, fail → data_gap); KO, DUK, Alphabet and Tesla are identical. **Federal Realty's 2011 does not move:** its per-share dividends are tagged from 2012, so 2011 precedes the first year with data and stays unpaid under this rule (a recorded-facts test pins that and tests the gap path on the same facts with 2016 removed). The only fix for that shape is another evidence concept (`DividendsCommonStockCash` shows a common dividend in some missing year for 3 of the 143 G4 fails with a no-data year; Federal Realty and Cigna would clear); not added, as it is a concept-map change beyond this decision.
**Amended 2026-10-12 (decided by Daniel):** `us-gaap:DividendsCommonStockCash` (the statement-of-equity line) is the last concept of the "dividends paid" fallback, with the same 0.5 size guard. It sits behind the per-share data and the cash-flow lines, so it only decides a year nothing else covers. Labels that moved over the index (fail → partial pass): **Federal Realty** (2011), **Cigna** (2016, a $0.04 dividend), **Cboe** (2011 to 2013) and **General Motors**. The GM change is doubtful: its 2011 to 2013 figures ($0.9 to $1.7B) are most likely preferred dividends tagged as common, since GM paid no common dividend before 2014; the fallback cannot tell, and G4 reads GM as a payer in those years. No other label moved.

### ADR-013 — Sector exceptions and ranking basis
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Context:** Some criteria are meaningless for certain companies.
**Decision:** Banks/insurers: G2 and B2 "not applicable". Utilities: G2 uses Graham's utility rule (debt ≤ 2× equity). Negative or near-zero equity: B1 "not meaningful". Criteria ranking uses % of applicable criteria met; data gaps reported separately.
**Trade-off:** Scorecard denominators vary by company; avoids penalising companies for tests that don't fit them.
**Amended 2026-10-08:** banks and insurers also get **B3 (stable margins)** and **B4 (owner earnings)** as "not applicable". The recorded JPM fixture has no operating income, D&A or capex tags, and both tests assume an industrial income statement. Bank-specific substitutes (e.g. pre-tax margin) were rejected: they would be the tool's own invented rules, not Graham's or Buffett's, and add scope. Banks and insurers are therefore scored on fewer applicable criteria; the varying denominator already handles this, and the scorecard states which tests were skipped and why.

### ADR-014 — S&P 500 constituents from a Wikipedia snapshot
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-07
**Context:** The official S&P constituent list is paid. Wikipedia's "List of S&P 500 companies" is free and includes CIK (direct EDGAR mapping) and GICS sector (drives ADR-013 exceptions).
**Decision:** (Built as ADR-053.) Save a dated CSV snapshot; never scrape at run time. Validate on load: ~503 rows, every CIK present and matching SEC's company ticker file, no duplicate symbols. ### ADR-025 — One checksummed response store behind three fetch modes (replay / live / record)
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-08
**Context:** The architecture says the cache is the only door to the outside world (ARCHITECTURE.md §1), and development must not make live calls. SEC also requires a declared User-Agent, which is personal and must not land in git. One 10-K can be 16 MB of HTML.
**Decision:** A single `ResponseStore` saves each raw response as gzip (fixed mtime, so identical content gives identical bytes) plus a `.meta.json` with URL, fetch time, SHA-256, size and content type. It backs both the git-ignored HTTP cache (`data/cache/`) and the committed `fixtures/`. `EdgarClient` has three modes: `replay` (fixtures only, a miss raises, never touches the network; the default for tests and development), `live` (cache, fetch on miss) and `record` (always fetch, write fixtures). Reads verify the checksum and fail on mismatch. Meta files record the URL only, never request headers. Standard library only; transport, sleep and clock are injectable so every test runs offline. Throttle is 0.15 s between requests (~6/s, under SEC's 10/s limit); retries 429/5xx/connection errors at 1, 2 and 4 s (honouring `Retry-After`); 403 and 404 fail immediately.
**Trade-off:** Fixtures are a frozen snapshot (latest 10-K and facts as of 2026-10-08) and go stale until re-recorded. Gains: reproducible tests, provenance (URL + hash) for every figure, no personal details in git, ~4 MB of repo for five companies despite 16 MB raw filings.

### ADR-026 — Annual period selection and fiscal-year labelling
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-08
**Context:** EDGAR company facts mix annual, quarterly and year-to-date figures from 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K filings. Each fact carries `fy`/`fp` fields, but they describe the *filing*, not the period: the prior-year comparative inside a FY2025 10-K also says `fy=2025`.
**Decision:** Keep only facts from form 10-K or 10-K/A. Duration facts must span 350–380 days (covers 52/53-week years); balance-sheet (instant) facts must fall within 7 days of the fiscal year end from `submissions.json` (`fiscalYearEnd`, MMDD). Each value is labelled by its period **end date**; `fy` and `fp` are never used (a test proves a comparative lands in the right year). All available years are kept; each scorer picks its own 10- or 15-year window.
**Trade-off:** Ignores 10-Q-only data (the latest quarter) and transition periods, so the series is annual and slightly behind. Gains: one unambiguous year label that cannot be shifted by how a company files comparatives.

### ADR-027 — Restatements: latest filed value, flagged, original kept
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-08
**Context:** The same period appears in several 10-Ks (as a comparative), and companies sometimes restate or recast it.
**Decision:** Use the value from the most recently filed 10-K for that period. If it differs from the earliest filed value by more than 0.5%, set `restated=true` and keep the original in `original_value`. Both values keep their accession, so either can be traced.
**Trade-off:** Scores use today's best-known number, not what investors saw at the time (no point-in-time view). Gains: consistent series, and restatements are visible instead of silent.

### ADR-028 — Concept map as reviewable config; per-year fallback; explicit derived values; never guess
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-08
**Context:** Companies tag the same measure with different XBRL concepts and change tags over time (GOOGL, TSLA and DUK all do). Hard-coding this in Python hides the judgement calls.
**Decision:** `config/concept_map.json` lists, per measure, the unit, period type and an ordered priority list of concepts, with a short reason per measure. For each fiscal year the first concept with a value wins, and the concept used is recorded on every value. If another listed concept disagrees by more than 1%, a note is added (no failure). Sums (e.g. D&A = depreciation + amortisation of intangibles) exist only where the map says so, need every part, and are marked `derived` with their source concepts. A measure/year with no match becomes a `Gap` with a reason (no listed concept reported, only non-annual data, unit mismatch). The normaliser reports gaps; whether a gap matters (e.g. a bank has no current assets, ADR-013) is the scorers' decision.
**Trade-off:** The map needs a human review per new company type, and fallback tags can mean slightly different things (e.g. net income including noncontrolling interests); the disagreement notes and the coverage report (`docs/coverage_report.md`) make that visible. Gains: no silent substitutions, and the judgement calls are in one reviewable file.

### ADR-030 — Depreciation alone as a flagged, conservative D&A fallback
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-08
**Context:** B4 owner earnings = net income + D&A − capex. Some companies stop tagging amortisation: Tesla reports only `Depreciation` from 2022, so the derived sum (ADR-028) is incomplete and B4 became a data gap for its four latest years.
**Decision:** When no listed D&A concept and no complete derived sum exists for a year, `Depreciation` alone is used, marked `derived` with a note "excludes amortisation (understates D&A)". Understating D&A lowers owner earnings, so the fallback errs on the conservative side, the same reasoning as using total capex as a proxy for maintenance capex (PROCESS §5, B4). Alphabet remains a gap for most years because it has no D&A or depreciation tags before 2021.
**Trade-off:** Mixes slightly different definitions within one series; mitigated by the per-value flag and the coverage report. Gains: Tesla is scored on real data instead of a gap, without ever overstating owner earnings.

### ADR-031 — Scoring windows and the as-of year
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-08
**Context:** Criteria are defined over "the last 10 years" (G4: 15), but companies report different numbers of years, and the normaliser reports gaps only after a series' first value.
**Decision:** The as-of year is the latest fiscal year with net income. The window is the 10 fiscal years ending there (G4: 15). Fiscal years are keyed by the year of the period end date (a 52/53-week year ending in the first days of January counts in the year before). "Years on record" are window years with a net income value (ADR-012 correction); G3 and G4 judge those years and label a short record "N yrs on record", but a net income hole *after* the first record year is a data gap. Every other multi-year test (G5, B1, B3, B4) needs every window year; a missing year is a data gap that names the years, wherever the series starts. B1 also needs the opening equity of the first year. Percent met = met / decided (pass, partial pass, fail); data gaps, not meaningful and not applicable are counted separately (ADR-013).
**Trade-off:** One missing year makes a test a gap instead of a judgement on nine years: stricter, but never a guess. Gains: one rule for all windows and gaps that tell the reader exactly which years are missing.

### ADR-032 — Stock-split adjustment of per-share values
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-08
**Context:** EDGAR keeps older per-share figures on the share basis of the filing that reported them. Alphabet's 2016 EPS is 27.85 and its 2020 EPS 2.93, because of the 2022 20:1 split; Tesla mixes three bases (5:1 in 2020, 3:1 in 2022). Unadjusted, G5 (EPS growth) and G6/G7 would be wrong by large factors.
**Decision:** `engine/splits.py` reads the split-ratio facts a company tagged (`StockholdersEquityNoteStockSplitConversionRatio` and `...Ratio1`) and keeps only splits that the normalised series confirms: some value was restated by the same ratio (or a product of consecutive ratios). Duke's 0.8708 merger exchange ratio has no such evidence and is rejected with a note; Alphabet's 2014 class C dividend likewise. EPS and dividends per share are divided, share counts multiplied, by the cumulative ratio of confirmed splits dated after the filing the value comes from. Each adjusted value records `split_factor` and `value_before_split`, and appears as `split_adjusted` in the scorers' flags. Split dates are approximate (the latest announcement/effective instant, else the earliest period end) because they are only compared with 10-K filing dates. Disagreement between ratio facts and restatements becomes a series note; nothing is guessed.
**Trade-off:** A split that no 10-K ever restated would go unadjusted (and noted). Gains: all per-share history on today's basis with the evidence in the trace, no external split table to maintain.

### ADR-034 — Edge-case rules in the scorers
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-08
**Decision:**
- **G4:** per-share dividends decide a year when the fact exists (a reported zero means unpaid); cash dividends paid are used only when per-share data is missing; neither means unpaid. This follows ADR-012's correction, where a one-off cash payment such as Alphabet's $47M in 2015 is not a dividend. A pass is a `partial_pass`, labelled "partial (15 of 20 yrs)" when the record covers all 15 years, else "N yrs on record". The source that decided each year is in the result.
- **G5:** a starting 3-year average EPS of zero or below is `not_meaningful` (averages shown, ratio withheld).
- **G6, G7:** with no price they are data gaps ("price not available (Finnhub, ADR-007)"). With a price, average EPS or book value per share of zero or below is a fail (no positive earnings or assets behind the price). P/E uses the 3-year average EPS in both. Book value per share is equity divided by split-adjusted diluted shares: a proxy, since EDGAR has no standard shares-outstanding concept in the map.
- **G2:** missing debt is a data gap, never "no debt". Utilities replace both tests with debt <= 2x equity.
- **B1:** ROE uses the average of opening and closing equity; any non-positive equity in the window is `not_meaningful`.
- **B2:** average net income of zero or below is a fail, with the figures shown.
- **B3:** the 10-year and 3-year margins are simple averages of yearly operating margins.
- **B4:** needs all 10 years of net income, D&A and capex, else a data gap listing the missing years. Years that use the depreciation-only fallback (ADR-030) are flagged.
**Trade-off:** Some choices are conservative (a gap rather than a guess; a fail for non-positive earnings). Gains: every label can be explained from the figures in the result without further assumptions.

### ADR-035 — 10-K text normalisation is the citation text
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** The qualitative agent must quote the filing, and the Critic checks each quote verbatim against it (ADR-019). A raw 10-K is inline-XBRL HTML (2–16 MB) whose markup, hidden XBRL header, non-breaking spaces and curly quotes would make "verbatim" depend on the parser.
**Decision:** `engine/filing_text.py` converts the HTML to plain text with the standard library's `html.parser`, deterministically: script, style and hidden inline-XBRL blocks (`ix:header`, `display:none`) are dropped; block elements become line breaks; a table row becomes one line with its non-empty cells joined by " | "; whitespace is collapsed; non-breaking spaces and curly quotes become plain ones. This text is THE citation text: every section and passage offset, and every quote, refers to it. It is cached under `data/cache/sections/` keyed by the document's SHA-256 and a text-format version (git-ignored, rebuilt on demand). The largest filing (Duke, 16 MB) converts in about a second.
**Trade-off:** Quotes are of the normalised text, so a quote differs from the printed filing in whitespace, quote marks and table layout; the passage id, accession and offsets still locate it exactly. Gains: one reproducible text for retrieval, citation and verification.

### ADR-036 — Section extraction: longest candidate, title fallback, explicit not_found
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** Items 1 (Business), 1A (Risk Factors) and 7 (MD&A) are the qualitative agent's evidence. Their headings appear twice (table of contents and body), differ in format (`ITEM 1A.RISK FACTORS`, `Item 1. | Business | 2`, `Items 1 and 2`) and some banks keep a cross-reference stub in Item 7 with the real MD&A elsewhere (JPMorgan: a 396-character stub; the MD&A follows the Item 15 index under repeating page headers).
**Decision:** `engine/sections.py` finds heading lines (`Item N`, including combined `Items 1 and 2`) and ends each section at the next heading of the following items (1: 1A/1B/1C/2; 1A: 1B/1C/2; 7: 7A/8). The candidate with the longest section wins (a table of contents entry is a few characters long) and must be at least 2,000 characters. Failing that, the section title alone on a line is tried the same way, ending at the next item heading or the auditor's report, and recorded as `method: title_fallback`. If nothing qualifies the section is `not_found` with a reason that states the longest candidates; nothing is guessed. Each section records status, method, offsets, length, accession and the document's SHA-256. All 15 test sections are found (JPMorgan's Item 7 by the fallback).
**Alternative considered:** the `edgartools` library already extracts sections. Rejected: a large dependency, heading rules we could not explain line by line, and no citation offsets into our own normalised text.
**Amendment 2026-10-12 (build 4a):** McDonald's Item 1 was not found because its 10-K has no "Item" headings in the body, only titles ("BUSINESS SUMMARY") and a cross-reference index at the end ("Item 1 | Business | Page 3"). Two changes, both in the title fallback only: the Item 1 title also matches "Business Summary" and "Business Overview", and a section found by title now also ends at the title line of Risk Factors or MD&A (never "Business", a common sub-heading). The second change fixes a latent fault the first exposed: McDonald's MD&A had run on through Risk Factors (134k characters, now 65k). Audited over the 25 analysed 10-Ks with `scripts/sections_audit.py` (no network): **74 of 75 items found before, 75 of 75 after**; the only differences are McDonald's Item 1 (not found → 22,651 characters) and Item 7 (133,983 → 65,003); every other section is byte-for-byte the same extent, including JPM's and Exxon's title-fallback Item 7. McDonald's analysis is therefore re-run in the refresh (its Item 1 and Item 7 passages change).
**Trade-off:** Heuristics tuned on five filings will meet layouts they cannot parse; the explicit `not_found` (and the sections report) make that visible instead of silently returning a table of contents. A long candidate that is really a different part (e.g. JPMorgan's 405,000-character MD&A) is accepted as the filing presents it.

### ADR-037 — Passages, BM25 retrieval and search terms as config
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** ADR-022 chose keyword search over three sections; it needs citable units and a ranking.
**Decision:** Each section is cut at paragraph boundaries into passages: short paragraphs are merged up to about 200 words, paragraphs over 350 words are split at sentence ends, tables count as paragraphs. `passage_id = "{accession}:{item}:{n}"`, and each passage stores its offsets so `document_text[start:end] == passage.text` exactly. `engine/retrieve.py` ranks passages with a small pure-Python BM25 (k1 = 1.5, b = 0.75; lowercase alphanumeric tokens, a short stopword list, a trailing plural "s" stripped, no stemming library). The agent's tools are `search(cik, query, items, k)` and `get_passage(passage_id)`, returning plain Pydantic objects with scores and matched terms for the trace. `config/search_terms.json` gives, per qualitative Buffett criterion, the items to search first and starting queries, with a reason each; the agent may rephrase.
**Trade-off:** Keyword search misses synonyms and rewards passages that mention many generic terms (see `docs/sections_report.md`: e.g. a receivables-factoring paragraph tops Coca-Cola's capital-allocation search). The starting queries are a baseline to evaluate, not tuned to make particular companies look good. ADR-022's upgrade path (a small local embedding model) stays open if the evaluation shows missed evidence.

### ADR-038 — LLM record/replay, the same pattern as the SEC data
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** Development, tests and the evaluation harness must not spend quota or depend on the network (ADR-025 for SEC data), and LLM output must be reproducible for the trace replay.
**Decision:** Every request is canonicalised (profile, model, messages, tools, response format, temperature, max tokens; never the API key or headers), hashed with SHA-256 and stored through `ResponseStore` as the provider's response plus the request and original latency: in `fixtures/llm/` (committed) for `record`, in `data/cache/llm/` (git-ignored) for `live`. `replay` (the default for tests) serves fixtures only and raises `LLMFixtureMissing` on a miss; it never touches the network and never writes the ledger. Temperature defaults to 0. The key is read from the environment only and redacted from errors; tests scan fixtures, traces and the ledger for it. Every call yields an `LLMCall` (tokens including thinking tokens, latency, actual and notional cost, replayed flag, the raw assistant message) and an automatic `llm_call` trace event. `complete_json` asks for JSON mode, validates against a Pydantic model and makes one repair attempt with the error; tool calls are returned in a provider-neutral shape.
**Trade-off:** A fixture only matches the exact same request, so changing a prompt means re-recording (a few free-tier requests). Gains: free, deterministic, offline tests of everything that uses the model. Found while recording: Gemini attaches `extra_content` (thought signatures) to tool calls and messages; the client keeps the raw assistant message so the agent loop can append it unchanged.

### ADR-039 — Budget guard and run ledger: Pacific-day quota, safety margin, paid-mode switch, caps
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** ADR-023 caps spending at $4 under a $5 Google cap and uses the free tier first. The free tier limits requests per minute and per day, and Google resets the daily count at midnight Pacific time.
**Decision:** `data/ledger/usage.json` (git-ignored) holds requests per profile per Pacific day, the last request time, total spend, notional spend and spend per company, so separate runs share them. Before every request (including retries, since every attempt counts against the quota) the guard (a) sleeps to stay under the profile's requests per minute, (b) refuses with `QuotaReached` ("resume after midnight Pacific") once today's count reaches the daily limit minus a margin of 10, and (c) allows a paid call beyond that only if `PAID_MODE_ENABLED=true` and the projected cost (prompt characters / 4 at the input price plus max tokens at the output price) keeps total spend under the $4.00 engine cap and the company under its $0.05 cap (all in `config/models.json`); otherwise `BudgetExceeded`. Calls inside the free quota are free even in paid mode. A 429 whose body names a daily quota stops the batch cleanly and marks the day exhausted in the ledger; per-minute 429s and 5xx errors retry with backoff (2, 4, 8 s; `Retry-After` honoured); a 400 is not retried. Each call records two costs: **actual** (money spent: zero on the free tier and for replays) and **notional** (tokens x the paid price), both shown in the demo.
**Trade-off:** Tokens per minute are not paced (requests per minute is the binding limit at our prompt sizes) and the cost projection is rough, so a paid call can overshoot its projection slightly; the $4 cap sits under Google's $5 cap for that reason. Gains: a hard, explainable ceiling and a batch that stops and resumes cleanly.

### ADR-041 — Qualitative agent: one loop per criterion, seeded first search in code, 4-turn cap
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** The "agentic" claim (ADR-018) rests on this loop, so its trace must read plan → tool use → decision → self-check. The free tier allows about 500 requests a day (ADR-023), and a model-chosen first search costs one request per criterion that almost always asks for what the starting queries already ask for.
**Decision:** `engine/agents/qualitative.py` runs four independent loops (smaller contexts, separate failures, a clean trace per criterion). Before the first model call, code runs `search_many` with the criterion's configured queries and items (k = 4) and puts the passages in the first user message; the trace records it as a tool call by `qualitative/<criterion>` marked as the seeded search. The model then has `search`, `get_passage` and `submit_finding`, temperature 0, at most 4 turns; on the last turn only `submit_finding` is offered and the message says so; no submission is "insufficient evidence" (`turn_cap`). If every item the criterion needs is `not_found` in the filing, the model is not called at all (`section_missing`, zero calls). Each assistant message is appended to the history unchanged (Gemini's thought signatures in `extra_content`), every tool call in a turn is answered in order, and tool errors go back to the model as tool messages, never exceptions. The measured cost is in `docs/qualitative_report.md`: 5–9 model calls per company, mean 7.0 (35 for the five test companies).
**Trade-off:** The model does not choose its first search, so the model shows less autonomy on turn 1; against that, the seeded search saves about one request per criterion (roughly half the calls) and is reproducible. The agent still searches freely afterwards, and did in 8 of the 20 recorded loops; `get_passage` was never used because the search results already carry the full passage text. A tool-using loop on a free tier with a small model also means some criteria end in `insufficient evidence` for mechanical reasons (see ADR-042 and `docs/evaluation_notes.md`).

### ADR-042 — Citation self-check inside the agent; the Critic still re-checks independently
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09 (the checking mechanics were replaced by sentence pointers in [ADR-046](#adr-046--citations-are-sentence-pointers-not-free-text-quotes-supersedes-adr-045); the principle and the Critic's independent re-check stand)
**Context:** Every verdict except "insufficient evidence" must quote and cite the filing (ADR-005, ADR-010). ADR-019 gives the Critic a rule check that quotes exist verbatim. A model asked for a quote paraphrases, splices sentences or adds punctuation often enough to matter (13 rejected submissions, in 8 of the 20 loops of the recorded run).
**Decision:** Before accepting `submit_finding`, code checks each quote: at most 60 words; contained, after collapsing whitespace only, in a passage retrieved during that criterion's loop (seeded, search or `get_passage`; a quote from a passage never retrieved is rejected even if it exists in the filing); starting and ending on a word edge; exact offsets computed in the normalised document text and `document[start:end]` round-trip checked. The stored quote is the document's own text, so whitespace differences vanish. A failed check returns a tool error that lists the bad quotes and why; one more submission is allowed (it may exceed the turn cap by one). Still invalid: "insufficient evidence", `invalid_citation`, rejected quotes kept in the trace. Malformed tool arguments get one corrective tool error, then `llm_output_error`. The Critic (ADR-019) still re-checks every quote independently: defence in depth, not duplication. The agent repairs; the Critic gates.
**Trade-off:** Strictness costs findings: when the repair fails, a perfectly sensible verdict is dropped (5 of 20 findings in the recorded run). Gains: nothing reaches the Critic or the site with a quote that is not in the filing, and the offsets are exact.

### ADR-043 — Rubric as config; the company's marketing language is a claim, not evidence
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** "Is management candid?" has no number, and a small model will say yes to any confident prose. What counts as evidence must be reviewable without reading code (as in ADR-028 and ADR-037).
**Decision:** Each criterion in `config/search_terms.json` now also carries `question`, `found_means`, `weak_means` and `insufficient_means`, and goes into the first user message verbatim. The system prompt is a short fixed constant, so request hashes are stable, and states that this is a research tool: no buy/sell/hold language, no price targets, no "investors should"; the rationale (at most two sentences, enforced as under 80 words) describes what the filing shows.
**Trade-off:** The rubric is a first draft and it shows: the recorded run gives "found" for candid management to all five companies, on routine MD&A explanations of declines (see `docs/evaluation_notes.md`). ### ADR-044 — Quota and budget errors propagate; they are never turned into "insufficient evidence"
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** A batch that hits the free daily quota must stop and resume from the ledger (ADR-039). An agent that caught the error and answered "insufficient evidence" would write a wrong-looking result for a company that was simply not analysed.
**Decision:** `QuotaReached`, `BudgetExceeded` and `LLMFixtureMissing` (and any other `LLMError`) leave `assess_qualitative` untouched; no partial `QualFindings` is returned, so the caller records the company as not done. Only conditions about the filing or the model's output become findings: `section_missing`, `turn_cap`, `invalid_citation`, `llm_output_error`.

### ADR-045 — Rejections say what to change; quotes are asked at about 40 words, accepted to 60
**Status:** Superseded by [ADR-046](#adr-046--citations-are-sentence-pointers-not-free-text-quotes-supersedes-adr-045) · 2026-10-09
**Context:** The first recording had 5 of 20 findings end in `invalid_citation`. The rejected quotes were mostly 61–74 words (the model cannot count words) or spliced two non-adjacent sentences; the model often resubmitted the identical text after a plain "not found".
**Decision:** The prompt asks for about 40 words, one contiguous stretch, and forbids joining sentences or changing punctuation; the limit stays 60. A rejection now says what to change: for an over-long quote that is real, a ready-made shorter contiguous quote from the same passage; for a quote that is not in the text, the longest leading part of it that is (a verified quote the model may submit as it stands) and what the text says next. Two prompt revisions cost three recordings (113 free-tier requests on 2026-10-09 in all; only the last, 35 requests, is kept in `fixtures/llm/`).
**Trade-off:** Feedback did not clearly help: findings ending in `invalid_citation` went 5, 4, 5 over the three recordings (the first before any change), so run-to-run variability is real and not yet measured. A model that ignores the offered fix still fails; a bigger model or a stricter output format would be the next step, not more prompting.

### ADR-046 — Citations are sentence pointers, not free-text quotes (supersedes ADR-045)
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** After two prompt revisions 5 of 20 findings still ended `invalid_citation` (ADR-045). In the traces 4 of the 5 rejected quotes ran past a bullet ("•") or a line break inside the passage and one was 63 words: the model reproduces text poorly across layout characters and cannot count words. The failure was in the design (asking a small model to retype text), not the wording of the prompt.
**Decision:** `engine/sentences.py` cuts every passage into sentences with exact document offsets (breaks at newlines, before bullets, after sentence-final punctuation followed by an uppercase letter, digit, quote or bracket, except after abbreviations and initials; a "sentence" over 80 words is cut at "; " or after word 60), stored on each `Passage`. Search and `get_passage` show a passage as a header plus numbered sentences. `submit_finding` takes `citations: [{passage_id, first, last}]` (1-based, inclusive, at most 3 consecutive sentences, at most 120 words), no quote text. Code builds the quote as `document[start(first):end(last)]`, so a quote mismatch is impossible by construction. Still enforced: the passage was retrieved in this criterion's loop, indices in range, the word cap; a bad pointer gets one corrective tool error naming the valid range, then `invalid_citation`. The free-text matching, prefix suggestions and quote re-attribution are removed. `Citation` keeps quote and offsets (derived) and adds `sentences: [first, last]`.
**Amendment 2026-10-10 (prompt v2, pending re-record):** the "at most 3 consecutive sentences" cap becomes "a contiguous range of at most 120 words", because the segmenter over-splits bullets and table rows (MetLife candid management failed twice on a 10- and a 9-sentence range of one risk-factor passage). The change is in the system prompt, the tool schema and `check_citations(max_sentences=None)`. It is written and tested but **not active**: `config/prompt_versions.json` keeps `qualitative: v1`, because a changed prompt is a changed request hash and the recorded fixtures (and every replay and test built on them) are v1. To activate: set v2, re-record the agent and the review step, prune orphans, refresh the gold `agent_verdict`s. Pending re-record is tracked in STATUS.
**Amendment 2026-10-12 (build 4a, prompt v2.1):** when a range is over the 120-word cap the tool error lists the word count of every sentence in it ("Words per sentence: [1] 40 words, [2] 90 words, …"), so the model can drop the right sentence instead of guessing. Checked against the three recorded rejections (O: 124 words in sentences 1-4; MetLife: 217 in 1-7 and 187 in 1-9): each message now names every sentence's words. Active from v2.1.
**Trade-off:** The quote boundary is the segmenter's, not the model's, so a cited span can include an extra clause or a bullet fragment, and a very long sentence can be cut mid-thought. Gains: zero mechanical citation failures (0 of 20 in the re-record, from 5 of 20), fewer output tokens (about 940 per company, from about 1,290), and a Critic that can check pointers exactly. The Critic still re-checks that each span supports its verdict (ADR-042).

### ADR-047 — Rubric tightened; "not assessed" is a separate outcome from "insufficient evidence"
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-09
**Context:** The first recording gave "candid management: found" to all five companies, on routine MD&A variance text (KO volume down 1%, TSLA revenue down 9%), and "durable advantage: found" to KO on a statement that its formulas are trade secrets (evaluation notes 7 and 8). Separately, a finding lost to a mechanical failure and a finding where the filing shows nothing both read "insufficient evidence" (note 10).
**Decision:** (1) Verdicts are now four: "evidence found", "evidence weak", "insufficient evidence" (the filing, as retrieved, does not show the criterion, or argues against it; the rationale says so) and "not assessed" (the system could not judge: `section_missing`, `turn_cap`, `invalid_citation`, `llm_output_error`, each with a one-line reason; no citations). The model cannot choose "not assessed"; code assigns it from the stop reason. (2) Rubric in `config/search_terms.json`: *candid management* is found only for management's own account of a setback attributed at least partly to its own decisions or execution, a missed target or guidance, or an acknowledged material weakness, restatement or write-down of something management chose; declines explained only by external factors (rates, currency, weather, regulation, the economy, mix, litigation outcomes) are weak even when quantified; queries added ("below our expectations", "we did not achieve", "lessons", "write-down of our investment", "restatement"); the 10-K is lawyered text and candour is better judged from shareholder letters (outside v1), which the model is told to say in the rationale when the verdict is not "found". *Durable advantage* is found only with a checkable specific (share or rank figure, regulated exclusive franchise, quantified scale or cost position, long-term contracts with terms, network size); an asset that exists or matters without a measure is weak. *Capital allocation* is found only with quantified actions in the fiscal year plus a stated policy, priority or rationale; plans only, actions without amounts, or amounts without rationale are weak. (3) For all criteria the rationale may state only what the cited sentences show. No extra code guard for that: the Critic's check 4 is meant to catch residual overreach, and evaluation note 9 stays open as its test case.
**Trade-off:** A stricter rubric trades recall for precision and is still a prompt, judged by a small model; the label still depends on run-to-run noise (`docs/qualitative_variability.md`). Keeping "not assessed" out of the model's hands means a model that wants to give up must say "insufficient evidence", a claim about the filing. Gains: the site and the Critic can tell "the filing does not show it" from "we could not tell".

### ADR-048 — Explainer writes placeholders, code writes the numbers (amends ADR-019 check 1)
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-10
**Context:** ADR-019 had the Critic check that every figure in an explanation matches the ScoreSet. A model that types "$47.94 billion" can type "$49.74 billion"; a check then catches it after the fact.
**Decision:** `engine/agents/explainer.py` asks the model for one or two sentences (at most 45 words) per pass / fail / partial / not-meaningful criterion, in one call per company, where any value from that criterion's `figures` (and the threshold) is a `{figure_key}` placeholder. Code renders it with the format named in `config/figure_formats.json` (usd_billions, usd_millions, ratio_2dp, percent_1dp, count, per_share_usd, years); a test enumerates every figure key of the 5 fixtures and fails if one has no format. Keys whose value is None are not offered. not_applicable and data_gap results get a templated line from their `reason`, with no model call. `Explanation` stores `raw` (with placeholders), `text` (rendered) and `placeholders_used`. The Critic's rule R1 therefore checks structure instead of arithmetic: placeholders valid for that criterion, no digits except a fiscal year of that result or a number inside its threshold string, and `text` reproducible from `raw` and the ScoreSet.
**Amendment 2026-10-10 (count placeholders, prompt v2):** year-list figures also get derived count placeholders (`{loss_year_count}`, `{profit_year_count}`, `{unpaid_year_count}`, `{paid_year_count}`, `{depreciation_only_year_count}`), rendered as numbers ("0 loss years"), so a sentence never has to print an empty list ("none years showing a loss"). Validation and rendering accept them now; the model is told about them, and the examples change, only under explainer prompt v2, which is inactive until the next LLM run (`config/prompt_versions.json`; v1 requests must stay as recorded). The 5 recorded results use only existing placeholders and re-render identically; the four that say "none years" stay as they are until then.
**Trade-off:** Less fluent phrasing: the first prompt produced "The 2.43 between the $2.66 and $1.09 exceeds ...", and it took explicit examples in the prompt (name the quantity before each placeholder, threshold last in parentheses) to get readable sentences; some still read stiffly. Gains: a wrong figure cannot be produced, and the recorded run had 0 explanation failures in 5 companies × 11 criteria.

### ADR-049 — Critic routing: failed items only, one retry to the owner, then a warning; it gates, it does not author
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-10
**Context:** ADR-010 gave the Critic a retry rule, ADR-019 a rules-first design. What it does to a failure was open.
**Decision:** `engine/agents/critic.py` runs rule checks first, none of which calls a model: R1 explanations, R2 citations (the 10-K is reloaded through the store and converted again; the document's sha256 must match the section record; then `text[start:end] == quote`, the span lies inside the cited passage and inside a found section of the stated item; it does not import the qualitative agent's pointer checker), R3 banned advice and valuation language in explanations and rationales (`config/banned_phrases.json`: regex with word boundaries, a reason each, an allowlist so "share buyback" and "customers buy" are not flagged). Then one model call per company (L1) over the found/weak findings with citations: the input is the criterion's question, the rubric line for the claimed verdict and the one below it, the quotes and the rationale, nothing else (no search history); the output is `supports_verdict` (yes / overstated / no), `unsupported_claims` and a reason. A finding fails L1 if the verdict is overstated or no, or if the rationale has claims the quotes do not show. `engine/agents/review.py::explain_and_verify` sends each failing item, and only that item, to its owner once: the Explainer re-runs for those criteria with the Critic's message; the qualitative loop for that criterion re-runs with the message added to its first user message. The retried items are re-checked (rules and L1). Still failing: the item is kept and marked `warning`. The Critic never rewrites a verdict or a sentence; the owner's latest output is what is kept. A retry that ends "not assessed" also counts as a warning (there is nothing left to confirm). `CriticReport` lists every check with item, status, owner and attempt, and a final status per item (passed, passed_after_retry, warning).
**Trade-off:** One retry is cheap and bounded, but in the recorded run it rarely helps: of 9 qualitative items sent back, 3 passed after retry and 6 ended as warnings, and the agent often restated the same unsupported claim. Gains: failures are visible instead of silently fixed, and a bad Critic cannot change a verdict.

### ADR-052 — Two repair paths for the Critic's findings; a failed repair keeps the original (amends ADR-049)
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-10
**Context:** In the first Critic run (ADR-049) the single retry fixed 0 of 3 verdict errors and cost 3.8 calls per company: told that a rationale had unsupported claims, the agent re-ran its whole loop and added new claims (DUK's retry added "$14,002 million" with no quote), and one retry hit the turn cap and left a finding "not assessed". Two different failures need two different repairs.
**Decision:** The Critic's model check labels each failed finding as a **verdict failure** (`supports_verdict` overstated or no, or a citation rule failure) or **rationale-only** (verdict supported, `unsupported_claims` not empty, or banned wording). Rationale-only → **rationale rewrite**: one `complete_json` call (agent `qualitative/<criterion>/rewrite`) that sees only the question, the verdict, the cited quotes and the problems to remove, and returns a new rationale (under 80 words) that states only what the quotes show; verdict and citations are untouched. Verdict failure → **constrained retry** (at most 3 turns): the first message gives the Critic's reason, the current verdict and its citations, and exactly two options, (a) search and cite sentences not cited before that show what was missing, or (b) submit a lower verdict; code refuses a higher verdict, and the same verdict without a newly cited sentence gets one tool error, then the retry ends. Both are re-checked (rules and the model check). A repair that fails the re-check, or a retry that ends not submitted, is dropped: the **original finding stays** and the item shows a warning (a retry can no longer turn a finding into "not assessed"). Each attempt is recorded in `CriticReport.repairs`; discarded attempts still count in the cost totals. The Critic's model check also reviews each written explanation (`agrees_with_label`, `reads_correctly`) in the same call; failures route to the Explainer retry. The rubric lines the Critic sees are the same `config/search_terms.json` text the agent was given.
**Amendment 2026-10-12 (build 4a, prompt v2.1): latest fiscal year.** Capital allocation and candid management carry a `year_rule` in `config/search_terms.json`: the verdict judges the company's latest fiscal year; sentences about the prior-year comparative count only as context and cannot be the evidence unless the filing says the same of the latest year (found in the review of the showcase batch, Exxon being the example). The agent sees the line in its first message and the Critic in each finding's payload (its system prompt says the rule is part of the criterion), so both judge the same question. The Critic previously had no prompt version; it now has one, v2 (everything recorded so far) and v2.1 (this rule).
**Trade-off:** Rewrites can only remove claims, they cannot add evidence; and a retry may lower a verdict the human would keep. Gains: in the recorded run every repair (5 of 5) passed the re-check, false alarms on correct verdicts fell from 6 to 2, and the repair step costs 1.2 calls per company instead of 3.8.

### ADR-053 — S&P 500 universe: one recorded Wikipedia snapshot, validated, one analysis per CIK (completes ADR-014)
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-10
**Context:** ADR-014 chose a dated Wikipedia snapshot; this builds it.
**Decision:** `scripts/snapshot_universe.py` fetches "List of S&P 500 companies" once through the MediaWiki API (`action=parse`, declared User-Agent), records the raw response in `fixtures/universe/` (checksummed store, like EDGAR) and parses the constituents table (by header name, not column position) to `data/universe/sp500_<date>.csv` (symbol, security, GICS sector and sub-industry, CIK, date added). `engine/universe.py` loads and validates it: 495 to 510 rows, no duplicate symbols, every CIK in SEC's `company_tickers` (the committed fixture) and consistent with SEC's CIK for that ticker, tickers normalised (`BRK.B` → `BRK-B`). Snapshot of 2026-10-10: 503 rows, 500 CIKs, all valid. Attribution: "Constituents: Wikipedia, as of <date> (CC BY-SA)", carried in every Result. `scripts/universe_report.py` records each company's SIC code once (`data/universe/sic_<date>.csv`) and writes `docs/universe_report.md`: evidence only, the engine still uses the SIC class (ADR-033). ### ADR-054 — Result schema 1.0 and the results layout
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-10
**Context:** ADR-020 makes the results JSON the contract between the engine and the static site.

### ADR-055 — Step degradation policy
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-10
**Context:** One company's failure must not stop a 500-company batch, and must not look like success.
**Decision:** `engine/orchestrator.py::analyse` runs plan → fetch → score → qualitative → explain_and_verify → report. Each step is timed and traced. A step that raises is recorded as failed (type and message) and noted in `Result.warnings`; a step whose input step did not complete is skipped with the reason ("depends on score, which did not complete"). Dependencies: score and qualitative need fetch; explain_and_verify needs both score and qualitative; report always runs. So a failed score still gets its qualitative findings, and a failed qualitative step still gets its scorecard (without explanations). An out-of-scope ticker gets a plan and a warning and makes no other call. Exceptions that propagate instead, because the batch must stop and the company must not be recorded as done (ADR-044): `QuotaReached`, `BudgetExceeded`, `LLMFixtureMissing`. Critic warnings and "not assessed" findings also appear in `Result.warnings`.
**Trade-off:** A partial result can be shown with fewer parts than a reader expects; the warnings and step records say exactly what is missing. Gains: one bad filing costs one company, not the run.

### ADR-056 — Batch, company ledger and PROGRESS.md (implements ADR-023 sections 1 and 2)

### ADR-061 — Revenue and dividends: skip a candidate that is a component of another
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-11
**Context:** The first run over the index (`docs/universe_scorecard_report.md`) showed revenue jumps of 3x or more in 35 companies. **Decision:** A measure can set `component_guard` (a fraction) in `config/concept_map.json`: for a fiscal year, a candidate worth less than that fraction of the largest candidate is a component, not the total, and is skipped, with a note. Revenue also gains two last-resort candidates for banks: `RevenuesNetOfInterestExpense` and net interest income plus noninterest income (a derived, noted sum). The five test companies are unchanged. Tested on recorded General Mills, Fifth Third, Goldman Sachs, Prologis and Trane facts and on synthetic cases.

Not fixed: a company where the component is the only candidate (Camden Property Trust: $0.01B of non-lease revenue), which needs a REIT revenue concept.

### ADR-062 — Rankings builder: the criteria ranking, its threshold and its caveat
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-11 (rules decided by Daniel; implements ADR-004)
**Context:** The numeric run (ADR-059) scored 498 companies with no model, enough for a first criteria ranking. ADR-004 requires the two rankings side by side with no commentary linking them; the analyst ranking needs Finnhub (ADR-007, ADR-021), whose permission is pending, and G6 and G7 need a price.
**Decision:** `engine/rankings.py` (`python -m engine.cli rankings`) reads `results/scores/<CIK>.json` (and the full result `results/<CIK>.json` where it exists: it sets `has_full_analysis` and `analysis_path`, and its scorecard stands in for a missing score file) for every company of the snapshot and writes `site_data/rankings.json`, validated against `schema/rankings.schema.json` (exported by `python -m engine.cli schema`; a test fails if the schema is stale). **Score** = met / decided, where met = pass + partial_pass and decided = pass + partial_pass + fail over G1 to G7 and B1 to B4 (not_applicable, not_meaningful and data_gap are left out). A company is **ranked only if decided ≥ 6**; the others are listed in an `insufficient_data` group with their counts and no rank. **Ties:** the exact fraction decides (no float comparison), then more criteria met, then fewer data gaps, then symbol, so every ranked company has a distinct rank. A row carries symbol, CIK, name, GICS sector and sub-industry, sector class, notes (the sector notes of ADR-057 and ADR-058 and the registrant note of ADR-063), caveats, rank, score, met, decided, data gaps, the label of every criterion, the as-of fiscal year and the full-analysis flag, so a site can filter by sector with no extra data. **Valuation caveat:** while a company's G6 or G7 is a data gap the row carries "Valuation criteria (G6, G7) pending price data." and so does the header; it goes away by itself when prices exist. **Analyst ranking:** an empty section (`ranking_by_analysts`: status pending, reason "pending Finnhub permission (ADR-007, ADR-021)", no rows). It is deliberately not called `analyst*`: the publish gate of ADR-021 matches that key pattern, and `write_rankings` applies the same gate, so Finnhub-shaped data in the file would fail the write unless `FINNHUB_PUBLIC_OK` is true. No sentence anywhere compares the two rankings. Companies with the ledger status `no_annual_report` (ADR-063) and companies with no result file are listed in `excluded` with the reason, never dropped. The header names the SEC fetch dates (earliest and latest `companyfacts` fetch), the constituents snapshot date, the disclaimer and the attribution lines (Wikipedia CC BY-SA, SEC EDGAR). The file has no wall-clock time: the same inputs write the same bytes.
**Trade-off:** A score over decided criteria rewards a short list of passes: a company with 6 of 6 outranks one with 9 of 10, and the many data gaps (G6 and G7 for everyone) make the denominators small and unequal; the row shows met, decided and gaps so the reader sees that. With G6 and G7 pending, banks and insurers (G2 and B2 to B4 not applicable, so at most 5 decided) can not reach 6 and all fall into the insufficient-data group until prices exist. A ranking from a git-ignored local run means the committed `site_data/rankings.json` is the evidence; its checks (schema, counts, order) run without the local files.
**Amended 2026-10-12 (decided by Daniel):** the sort key is the **lower bound of the 95% Wilson score interval** for met / decided (z = 1.96), and the threshold is **decided ≥ 5**, so banks and insurers (5 decided criteria while G6 and G7 are pending) are ranked. Ties: higher met, then fewer data gaps, then symbol. A row keeps `met`, `decided`, `percent_met` and data gaps and its `score` is now the Wilson lower bound to 3 decimals. Why: the raw share favoured short lists (6 of 6 above 9 of 10 and 14 companies at 100%); the lower bound asks how much evidence stands behind the share. It reduces the effect rather than removing it: 9 of 9 gives 0.701, 6 of 6 gives 0.610, 9 of 10 gives 0.596, 5 of 5 gives 0.566, so a 9 of 10 now beats 5 of 5 but 6 of 6 still edges out 9 of 10. Effect: 477 ranked, 21 insufficient data, 2 excluded (was 406 / 92 / 2); the top 10 is unchanged because the leaders are all at 100% and the lower bound only separates them by list length.

**Amended 2026-10-12 (default rules, Daniel may veto):** (1) **Pending valuation:** G6 and G7 that are data gaps only because no price exists are labelled `pending` in the rankings (a distinct mark on the site), are not data gaps and are not counted as decided; the data-gap count and the tie-break use real gaps only. (2) **Main ranking: decided ≥ 7.** (3) **Banks and insurers get their own ranking** ("Banks and insurers (fewer tests apply)", `ranked_financials`), the same Wilson score, threshold **4** decided tests, with their own rank numbers; every other company under the threshold stays in `insufficient_data`. Each row carries its `group`. Before → after (498 scored): threshold 5 for all, 477 ranked and 21 insufficient; now **371 in the main ranking, 41 banks and insurers ranked, 86 insufficient** (31 companies with 5 decided tests, 25 with 6, and the rest with fewer: mostly 2020s listings and REITs without capex). New main top 10: AMAT, FAST, GWW, WST (9 of 9), SNA (8 of 8), EME, EXPD, GRMN, WSM (7 of 7), ACN (8 of 9, 0.565); the banks and insurers lead with GL, IBKR and PGR (5 of 5, 0.566) then AXP (4 of 4, 0.510).

### ADR-063 — Registrant successors and companies without an annual report
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-11 (Daniel's decision on the handling; the Exxon evidence checked on EDGAR the same day)
**Context:** Three snapshot CIKs had no 10-K facts and failed the numeric run (ADR-059). Honeywell Aerospace (HONA, added 2026-06-29) and Vylor (VYLR, 2026-10-01) are new registrants from spin-offs (Form 10-12B in their filing histories, no 10-K). ExxonMobil Holdings Corp (CIK 0002115436) is different: its history holds a Form **8-K12B** filed 2026-07-01 (accession 0001193125-26-291990, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2115436/000119312526291990/d71068d8k12b.htm) "for the purpose of establishing ExxonMobil Holdings Corporation as the successor registrant of ExxonMobil's common stock pursuant to Rule 12g-3(a)" after a redomiciliation merger effective 2026-07-01. The predecessor, Exxon Mobil Corp (CIK 0000034088), filed the FY2025 10-K on 2026-02-18 (0000034088-26-000045) and the 10-Q of 2026-08-03; the successor has filed no 10-K.
**Decision:** (1) `config/registrant_successors.json` maps a successor CIK to its predecessor CIK(s) with the evidence filing (form, accession, URL, quote), the reason and the visible note. `EdgarClient.source_for` redirects `companyfacts` and `latest_filing` to the predecessor while the successor's own submissions list no 10-K; once they do, the successor is read directly with no code change. (2) A company with no 10-K and no predecessor gets the ledger status **no_annual_report**, not "failed": the message is "No annual report (10-K) filed yet; listed since <date_added>.", the numeric score file has `status: no_annual_report` and no score, the full path skips every step with that reason, and the company is out of every ranking with that reason. **Trade-off:** For Exxon the figures end at FY2025 and the filing is the predecessor's, which is exactly what the note says; the redirect trusts the config, so a wrong entry would put another company's figures under a CIK (hence the evidence fields and the test that the evidence filing is in the successor's own history). Gains: the largest US energy company is scored and traceable instead of failed, and no heuristic guesses at successors.

### ADR-064 — Coverage recovery: validated derived operating income, REIT lease revenue, and the capex concepts that were not added
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-11 (the derived operating income was allowed by Daniel on 2026-10-11 under the condition "same filing and period, no different scope"; the scope check below is how that condition is met)
**Context:** The numeric run (ADR-059, `docs/evaluation_notes.md` item 20) left B3 a data gap for 120 companies (operating income not tagged by 82), B4 for 101 (capex not tagged by 41, 12 of them REITs) and Camden Property Trust with $0.01B of revenue. Daniel allowed a derived `Revenues − CostsAndExpenses` for operating income and asked to check which concepts the companies report before choosing.
**Decision:** (1) **Operating income.** The concept map gets a `{"difference": [A, B], "validate_against": C}` entry (`engine/normalise.py`, flagged `derived`, source concepts recorded, both parts from the same filing and the same fiscal period). Checking showed that `Revenues − CostsAndExpenses` equals the reported operating income in 1,151 of 1,315 company-years of the 131 non-financial companies that tag all three, but that of the 14 non-financial companies with no `OperatingIncomeLoss` that tag both, 7 (Chevron, Bristol-Myers Squibb, HCA, Textron, Ventas, Welltower, Arthur J. Gallagher) have a difference equal to pre-tax income in every year because their costs include interest and other non-operating items: a different scope. The companies that lack the tag are exactly the ones where the difference is least likely to be operating income, so the difference is accepted for a company **only when it is proven**: in at least 2 periods where all three concepts exist, A − B equals the reported value within 1% in every one, and only for periods from the first proven one onward (Ford started tagging in 2017; its 2007 to 2016 differences are not used). That fills the years after a company stopped tagging the concept (Boston Properties and Public Storage from FY2018) and nothing else. (2) **REIT revenue.** `OperatingLeaseLeaseIncome` and `OperatingLeasesIncomeStatementLeaseRevenue` are the last two revenue candidates; the component guard of ADR-061 skips Camden's $0.01B fee line and its rental income ($1.57B in 2025) becomes revenue. The guard now notes only a skip that changed the choice (a small candidate behind the chosen one is no finding). (3) **Capex: nothing added.** Measured against `PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment` where a company reports both: `PaymentsForCapitalImprovements` median 0.38 of it, `PaymentsToAcquireOtherProductiveAssets` 0.05, `PaymentsToAcquireOtherPropertyPlantAndEquipment` 0.92 with 37% of years below half, `PaymentsForConstructionInProcess` (utilities) about 9 times; the real-estate lines are acquisitions, development or improvements, each one part of a REIT's capex. None has total-capex meaning, and using a part alone would overstate owner earnings, the opposite of the conservative proxy intended for REITs. The one combined REIT concept, `PaymentsToAcquireAndDevelopRealEstate`, was tried and removed: it moved no REIT (their D&A or history is missing too) and one software company (Workday, a campus-development line). The reasons are recorded in the concept map. Effect over the index (every moved label): B3 data_gap → pass for Boston Properties, → fail for Public Storage; G1 data_gap → pass for VMRK, Vivmark Residential (a REIT that tags no revenue concept at all) and fail → pass for Camden. Companies without a score gained: 2 (B3), 2 (G1); B4 unchanged at 101. The five test companies are unchanged (byte-identical results).
**Trade-off:** The recovery is small on purpose: the rule that would have recovered about 50 more B3 scores (derive for every company) would have mixed pre-tax income into an operating-margin test for the companies where it matters most. The cost is that large names (Chevron, Eli Lilly, Pfizer, Merck, IBM) stay B3 data gaps; their filings simply do not present an operating income line.

### ADR-065 — Static site stack and the publish pipeline
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-12

### ADR-072 — Prompt-perturbation stability and the borderline mark (build 4a)
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-12
**Context:** The drop from 19/20 to 14/20 (ADR-069) came from wording changes that should not matter, so some verdicts depend on the wording more than on the filing. A reader looking at "evidence weak" cannot tell a settled judgement from a coin flip.
**Decision:** Each qualitative criterion's question and its three verdict definitions are reworded twice, keeping the meaning (`config/qualitative_paraphrases.json`, variants A and B; items, queries, the year rule and the rationale note stay as they are). The agent and the Critic are run again on the same 10-K with each variant, live, one run id per variant (`<SYMBOL>-live-a`, `-b`), results stored apart in `results/stability/{a,b}/` and never replacing the main results. The variant runs reuse the main run's numeric explanations (they do not depend on the qualitative wording), so a variant costs the qualitative loops and the Critic only. Per finding, the verdicts of main, A and B give `stability: {status, verdicts: {main, a, b}}` (Result schema 1.2, additive): **stable** when the assessed verdicts are all equal, **borderline** when any differ. A verdict of "not assessed" is a system outcome, not a judgement: it is kept in the record but does not count, and with fewer than two assessed verdicts no status is given. The company page shows a Borderline mark next to a verdict that changed, one sentence ("When the question was reworded, the verdict came out as weak and insufficient in two other runs.") and the three verdicts in the evidence pane; the trace page still shows the main run, and the rankings do not use qualitative verdicts. `scripts/stability_report.py` writes `docs/stability_report.md` (flip rates overall and per criterion, pairwise rates, whether the dev-set disagreements are the borderline findings, the paraphrases side by side for review).
**Result (all three runs, 25 companies):** 33 of 96 findings with a status are borderline (34%); 25 move one level and 8 move two. By criterion: understandable business 0 of 23 (0%), durable advantage 11 of 25 (44%), candid management 18 of 25 (72%), capital allocation 4 of 23 (17%). **All six differences between the agent and the human labels under prompt v2 are borderline findings, as are all five under v2.1; none of the 12 stable dev findings disagrees with the human label** (Fisher exact one-sided p = 0.004 on 20 findings of five companies: read the counts, not the p-value).
**Amendment 2026-10-12 (decided by Daniel): criterion reliability.** From the stability data each qualitative criterion's flip rate is computed over all analysed companies (findings with at least two assessed verdicts: `changed` of `compared`). A criterion at or above the threshold in `config/reliability.json` (30%) gets `reliability: "low"` and its basis on every finding in the Result (schema 1.3, additive), and the company page says, next to the criterion and in the evidence pane: "Low reliability: across all analysed companies, this verdict changed when the question was reworded for n of m companies." Computed by `attach` (never typed in); how-it-works lists every criterion's rate from `data/stability.json`. Durable competitive advantage (44%) and candid management (72%) are marked; capital allocation (17%) and understandable business (0%) are not. The rate describes the criterion, not a company: a verdict on a marked criterion can still be right, and an unmarked criterion can still be wrong for a given company.
**Trade-off:** Three times the qualitative and Critic calls once per refresh (measured: a variant run is 227 and 250 requests for 25 companies, 9.1 and 10.0 per company, against 274 for the main run), kept out of the main run economics because a refresh may skip it. The mark says the verdict depends on the wording; it does not say which wording is right. Two paraphrases is a small sample of the possible wordings, written by the same person (and model) who wrote the rubric, so "stable" means stable under these rewordings, not under every one. Gains: uncertainty a reader can see, and a number (the flip rate) that says how much of the 14-to-19-of-20 spread is wording.

### ADR-074 — Threshold sensitivity method (build 4b; completes the ±20% test promised in ADR-011)
**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-16
**Context:** The Buffett thresholds are judgement calls (ADR-011) and the Graham ones were adapted, so the useful question is how many labels change if a threshold is a little different.
**Decision:** The scorers read their numeric thresholds from `engine/score/thresholds.py` (defaults equal to the published values; `override` is a context manager that restores them). `eval/sensitivity.py` normalises and split-adjusts every scored company once from the local SEC cache (no network, no model), scores it at the baseline, then moves each threshold on its own down and up by 20% and scores everything again, counting the companies whose label changes per criterion and in which direction (to fail, to pass). A threshold that is a count of years (B1's 7 years, B4's 8 years) is rounded to the nearest whole year (6 and 8, 6 and 10). Twelve thresholds are covered; G6 and G7 compare a price with earnings and have no labels until price data exists, so they are listed as not testable. The result is frozen in `eval/sensitivity_results.json` and a test recomputes it from the cache. **Headline over 498 companies:** the worst-case share of decided labels that change is B3 28% (the 90%-of-average margin ratio raised to 108% moves 96 of 341), B4 15% (the 10-of-10-years version moves 52 of 357), G5 13%, B1 10%, B2 9%, G2 3% (the utility variant moves 10 of its 27 companies) and G1 0%. B3, B4 and G5 are the threshold-sensitive tests: a company near the line there should not be over-read.
**Trade-off:** Moving one threshold at a time hides interactions (B1's ROE level and its year count together), and 20% is a convention, not a measured uncertainty; rounding a year count makes the lower and higher steps unequal. Gains: a deterministic, reproducible table that says which tests are fragile, at no model cost.

## Planned for v2 (only after v1 ships)
Not numbered ADRs yet; each gets the next ADR number when it is built.

### V2-1 — Read-only MCP server over the published results
**Status:** Planned · 2026-10-09
**Context:** A platform other tools can use is more useful than another web page. MCP lets a person's own AI desktop app (Claude Desktop confirmed for local servers; Gemini and ChatGPT support to be checked at build time) call external tools.
**Decision:** After v1 ships, publish a small local MCP server (a Python package) that reads the versioned results JSON (ADR-020) from the static site and exposes read-only tools, e.g. `get_scorecard(ticker)`, `get_rankings()`, `get_criterion_detail(ticker, criterion)`, `get_trace(ticker)`. The user's own AI does the reasoning, so it costs us $0: no LLM calls, no keys, no hosting. Rules: pre-computed results only, no tool triggers an engine run; every response carries the disclaimer, sources and as-of date; tool descriptions say "research data, not financial advice"; no verdict language in any payload (ADR-003); Finnhub-derived fields only if the ADR-021 publish gate allows them (serving them over MCP is redistribution). A hosted remote server is a later option (adds hosting and auth).
**Trade-off:** We cannot control how the connected AI phrases our data (it may turn a scorecard into "buy this"); mitigated by what the payload contains and says, not by controlling the client. Gains: the results schema working as an API that other tools can call, for $0. Priority: first v2 item, ahead of V2-2.

### V2-2 — Cross-provider model comparison on Amazon Bedrock
**Status:** Planned · 2026-10-09
**Context:** Bedrock is a widely used way to run models from several providers behind one API. ADR-015 already plans a free Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite vs hosted Gemma 4 31B comparison on the evaluation harness, and ADR-040 makes the model a config profile.
**Decision:** After v1 ships and the free Gemini/Gemma comparison exists, add Bedrock as one more provider and run the same evaluation harness across 3–4 models from 2 providers, reporting citation accuracy, Critic pass rate, tokens and cost per analysis. Bedrock's native interface is the Converse API, so this likely needs a thin adapter behind the existing client interface (to be confirmed at build time). Containment: the 5 test tickers only (ADR-024), small models, outputs recorded as fixtures (ADR-038), and a separate Bedrock cap in the ledger (ADR-039).
**Trade-off:** Bedrock bills per token with a card on file, and AWS Budgets alerts rather than hard-stops, which breaks the hard-cap rule unless contained as above; the $5 budget is already committed to Gemini (ADR-023), so a small separate allowance (about $1–2) or a lower Gemini cap must be decided before building. Expected cost is cents; confirm current pricing and any new-account credits first. Gains: results from several providers behind one interface, and a live check of the provider-agnostic design.


### V2-3 — Cross-company "Ask the filings": hybrid retrieval over a vector index of S&P 500 10-K passages
**Status:** Planned · 2026-10-09
**Context:** ADR-022 deferred embeddings and named cross-company search as a revisit trigger. Questions such as "which companies cite supplier concentration in China as a risk?" span ~100k+ passages with varied wording, where keyword search struggles. The v1 qualitative agent is already agentic RAG (retrieval plus cited generation); this adds semantic retrieval at scale.
**Decision:** After v1 ships (the S&P 500 batch produces the 10-K sections anyway), embed the existing passages, keeping the same `passage_id` and offsets so every answer still cites text the Critic can verify verbatim (ADR-019, ADR-035); no re-chunking. Retrieval is hybrid: BM25 plus vectors, merged with reciprocal rank fusion. Starting choices, to confirm at build time: a small local embedding model (about 384 dimensions, via an ONNX runtime such as fastembed rather than PyTorch) and a file-based vector store (LanceDB, Chroma or sqlite-vec), all $0. Public site: pre-computed answers to a set of example questions with citations; live questions only behind access codes (ADR-017); the same index exposed as a `search_filings` tool in the V2-1 MCP server, so the user's own AI does the reasoning at no LLM cost to us. Report recall@k for BM25, vectors and hybrid on a labelled question set.
**Trade-off:** An index of roughly 150–250 MB is too large for the static site as one file, so live search needs a backend (e.g. a free-tier Postgres with pgvector; check limits and any card requirement before signing up) or stays local. A new dependency and an index to rebuild when filings change. Gains: a measured retrieval comparison ("hybrid found X% more evidence than keyword search") rather than "used a vector DB".
